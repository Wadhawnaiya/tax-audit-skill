# AY 2026-27 (FY 2025-26) Numeric Thresholds, Rates & Due Dates

**Authoritative source for this skill.** Where any clause-guide file states a figure that conflicts with this
file, trust this file. Compiled and web-verified for Assessment Year 2026-27 / Previous Year 2025-26 (1-Apr-2025
to 31-Mar-2026), under the Income-tax Act, 1961, Section 44AB, Forms 3CA/3CB-3CD. Confirmed current as of this
skill's build date (Jul-2026), due dates re-verified 20-Nov-2026; refresh after the next Finance Act (Budget, typically Feb) if this file is more
than a year old — see the update checklist at the end.

## 1. Section 44AB — tax audit applicability thresholds

| Category | Standard threshold | Enhanced threshold | Condition for enhanced threshold |
|---|---|---|---|
| Business (turnover) | Rs. 1 crore | Rs. 10 crore | Cash receipts ≤ 5% of total receipts **and** cash payments ≤ 5% of total payments during the year (both conditions, not either) |
| Profession (gross receipts) | Rs. 50 lakh | — (no enhanced/cash-light threshold for professions) | n/a |

Plus the mandatory-audit triggers regardless of turnover:
- **44AD(4)**: assessee opted for presumptive taxation (44AD) in an earlier year, doesn't opt for it in the current year within the 5-year lock-in, and total income exceeds the basic exemption limit → audit mandatory.
- **44ADA**: professional declares income below 50% of gross receipts and total income exceeds the basic exemption limit → audit mandatory.

Source: ClearTax, TaxBuddy, LegalSuvidha, incometaxindia.gov.in Section 44AB page (cross-checked, consistent across sources).

## 2. Presumptive taxation schemes

| Section | Who | Turnover/receipts limit | Enhanced limit (cash-light) | Deemed profit rate |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 44AD | Business (resident individual/HUF/partnership firm, not LLP) | Rs. 2 crore | Rs. 3 crore if ≥95% of receipts are digital | 8% (6% for digital receipts/payments) |
| 44ADA | Specified professionals (resident individual/partnership firm, not LLP) | Rs. 50 lakh | Rs. 75 lakh if cash receipts ≤5% of total | 50% |
| 44AE | Goods-carriage transporters (owning ≤10 vehicles at any time in the year) | n/a (per-vehicle) | n/a | Heavy goods vehicle (>12,000 kg gross weight): Rs. 1,000 per ton of gross/unladen weight per month or part-month owned. Other vehicles: Rs. 7,500 per vehicle per month or part-month owned. |

Note: 44AD/44ADA enhanced limits require checking cash receipts against the whole year's receipts, not just a
sample — verify from the bank statement + cash book, not assumption.

## 3. Section 40A(3) / 40A(3A) — cash payment disallowance

- Rs. 10,000 per day, per payee, for revenue expenditure → disallowed under 40A(3) if exceeded and paid in cash/bearer cheque (not account payee).
- Rs. 35,000 per day, per payee, for payments to a transport operator for plying/hiring/leasing goods carriages.
- Rule 6DD lists specific exceptions (payments to RBI/banks/government, no banking facility available at the place, payment on a bank holiday, payment by book adjustment to certain agents, etc.) — check these before disallowing.
- These figures are long-standing and were **not** changed by Finance Act 2025.

## 4. Section 43B(h) — MSME payment time limit

- Statutory time limit under Section 15 of the MSMED Act, 2006: **15 days** if there is no written agreement, or **up to 45 days** if a written agreement specifies a longer period (45 days is the maximum permissible even by agreement).
- Effective from FY 2023-24 onward (inserted by Finance Act 2023).
- **Unlike every other category under Section 43B, there is no return-filing-due-date grace period for 43B(h)** — if unpaid beyond the MSMED time limit as at year end, it is disallowed for that year regardless of whether it's paid before the return is filed. (Already verified in detail by the clauses-18-to-26.md research — treat that file's account as authoritative for the mechanics; this entry just anchors the headline figures.)
- Note: this rule applies only to dues to suppliers registered as **Micro or Small** enterprises under Udyam registration — Medium enterprises are excluded from 43B(h) (though may still be covered by ordinary commercial payment terms/interest under Section 16 of the MSMED Act).

## 5. Section 36(1)(va) — employee welfare fund contributions

- Employee's own contribution (PF/ESI/superannuation/other welfare fund, deducted from salary) must be deposited by the **due date under the respective Act** (e.g. 15th of the following month for EPF) to be allowable.
- Per the Supreme Court's ruling in *Checkmate Services Pvt. Ltd. vs CIT* (2022), there is **no grace period** — even a one-day-late deposit results in **permanent disallowance** of that contribution. This is distinct from the employer's own contribution, which falls under ordinary Section 43B (allowable if paid before the return filing due date).

## 6. TDS rate table — sections most relevant to SME/business clients, FY 2025-26

Budget 2025 (Finance Act 2025) rationalized several TDS thresholds effective **1 April 2025** — several older
"threshold as of last year" assumptions are now out of date. Verified via ClearTax, IndiaFilings, Trilegal, B S
Sridhar & Co., and cross-checked for consistency across sources.

| Section | Nature of payment | Threshold (FY 2025-26) | Rate |
|---|---|---|---|
| 192 | Salary | Basic exemption limit (slab-based) | Slab rate per regime opted by employee |
| 194A | Interest (other than on securities) | Rs. 50,000 (banks/co-op societies/post office); Rs. 1,00,000 for senior citizen payees; Rs. 10,000 in other cases | 10% |
| 194C | Payments to contractors | Rs. 30,000 single payment / Rs. 1,00,000 aggregate in the year (unchanged) | 1% (individual/HUF payee), 2% (others) |
| 194H | Commission or brokerage | Rs. 20,000 (raised from Rs. 15,000) | 2% (reduced from 5% by an earlier amendment effective Oct-2024) |
| 194-I | Rent | Rs. 50,000 per month or part of a month (replaced the old Rs. 2,40,000 per year limit from 1-Apr-2025). Sec 194-IB (rent paid by individuals/HUF not liable to tax audit): Rs. 50,000 per month | 10% (land/building/furniture), 2% (plant & machinery); 194-IB 2% |
| 194J | Fees for professional or technical services | Rs. 50,000 (raised from Rs. 30,000) | 10% (professional services), 2% (technical services, call centre operations, certain royalty) |
| 194Q | Purchase of goods | Rs. 50 lakh aggregate purchases from one seller in the year | 0.1% on amount exceeding Rs. 50 lakh |
| 194T | **New section** — payments by a firm/LLP to its partners (salary, remuneration, bonus, commission, interest) | Rs. 20,000 aggregate in the year | 10% |
| 206C(1H) | TCS on sale of goods | **Omitted/removed effective 1 April 2025** — do not apply for FY 2025-26 | — |

**Important for this demo/skill's clause 34 workpapers:** since 206C(1H) no longer applies from FY 2025-26, don't
flag its absence as a compliance gap for sellers — it simply doesn't exist anymore for this year. Section 194Q
(buyer-side TDS on purchase of goods) continues unaffected and is now the sole mechanism covering that ground.
Section 194T is new and easy to miss for partnership/LLP clients specifically — check partner
salary/remuneration/interest ledgers against Rs. 20,000 aggregate threshold whenever the client is a firm or LLP.

Sources: cleartax.in/s/tds-and-tcs-changes-from-april-2025, indiafilings.com TDS rule changes April 2025,
trilegal.com Budget 2025 TDS/TCS rationalisation note, bssridhar.com TDS/TCS rate chart FY 2025-26. These
figures were consistent across all four independent sources checked.

## 7. Sections 269SS / 269T / 269ST

- **269SS** (acceptance of loan/deposit/specified sum): Rs. 20,000 — must be by account payee cheque/bank draft/ECS/other prescribed electronic mode, not cash.
- **269T** (repayment of loan/deposit/specified advance): Rs. 20,000 — same mode restriction.
- **269ST** (receipt restriction, broader than just loans): Rs. 2,00,000 — no person shall receive an amount of Rs. 2 lakh or more in aggregate from a person in a day, or in respect of a single transaction, or in respect of transactions relating to one event/occasion, otherwise than by account payee cheque/draft/ECS/other prescribed electronic mode.
- These figures are long-standing and unchanged by Finance Act 2025.
- Note: per the clauses-27-to-35.md research, a new granular nature/mode-of-transaction coding requirement was introduced for clause 31 reporting effective AY 2025-26 (CBDT Notification 23/2025) — refer to that file for the coding detail; this entry is limited to the monetary thresholds.

## 8. Section 56(2)(x) — inadequate consideration

- Rs. 50,000 — receipt of money, immovable property, or specified movable property without consideration, or for consideration less than fair market value/stamp value by more than this amount (and subject to the standard tolerance bands under the relevant sub-clauses), is taxable as income from other sources. Unchanged.

## 9. GAAR (Sections 96–98) applicability threshold

- Rs. 3 crore aggregate tax benefit in a year, per Rule 10U(1)(a) — below this, GAAR provisions don't apply, per CBDT Circular 7/2017. (Independently confirmed by the clauses-27-to-35.md research agent as well — consistent.)

## 10. Section 94B — interest limitation (thin capitalisation)

- Applies only where interest/similar expenditure paid to an associated enterprise (or AE-guaranteed debt) by an Indian company or a PE of a foreign company exceeds **Rs. 1 crore** in the year.
- Disallowance: interest exceeding 30% of EBITDA (or interest paid to AE, whichever is lower) is disallowed and carried forward up to 8 assessment years.
- Unchanged by Finance Act 2025.

## 11. Section 286 — Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR) threshold

- Consolidated group revenue threshold: **Rs. 6,400 crore** (per Rule 10DB, Notification 31/2021, aligned to the OECD EUR 750 million benchmark). Independently confirmed by the clauses-36-to-44.md research agent — consistent.

## 12. Section 44AA — mandatory books of account thresholds

| Assessee type | Trigger |
|---|---|
| Specified professions (legal, medical, engineering, architectural, accountancy, technical consultancy, interior decoration, authorised representative, film artist, company secretary, IT professional) | Must maintain prescribed books regardless of income/turnover level, unless opting for 44ADA presumptive taxation |
| Individual/HUF (non-specified business/profession) | Books required if income > Rs. 2,50,000 **or** turnover/gross receipts > Rs. 25,00,000 in any of the preceding 3 years (or expected to exceed, for a new business) |
| Other assessees (firms, companies, etc., non-specified business/profession) | Books required if income > Rs. 1,20,000 **or** turnover > Rs. 10,00,000 in any of the preceding 3 years |

**Flagged as not fully confirmed:** some current sources claim a cash-light Rs. 10 crore variant of the "other
assessees" turnover figure, apparently borrowing the 44AB enhanced-threshold logic — this looks like probable
conflation with Section 44AB rather than a genuine 44AA change and should **not** be relied on without checking
the bare Act text directly if it matters to a specific client. Default to the Rs. 10,00,000 / Rs. 1,20,000 figures
above.

## 14. New tax regime (Section 115BAC) — individuals/HUF/AOP/BOI, FY 2025-26

- **115BAC is the default regime** unless the assessee opts out (relevant mainly for clause 8a-equivalent regime disclosures and any individual-assessee context, e.g. Form 16 cross-checks).
- Section 87A rebate: full rebate for taxable income up to **Rs. 12 lakh** under the new regime (rebate up to Rs. 60,000), with marginal relief above that. Standard deduction of Rs. 75,000 for salaried individuals under the new regime means effectively nil tax up to ~Rs. 12.75 lakh gross salary.
- Old regime rebate remains capped at taxable income up to Rs. 5 lakh (rebate up to Rs. 12,500).
- Source: cleartax.in, tax2win.in, incometaxindia.gov.in FAQ page on Section 87A for FY 2025-26 — consistent across sources.

## 15. Key due dates for AY 2026-27

| Event | Statutory date | **Extended date (CBDT press release 28-Sep-2026 / Circular No. 7/2026)** |
|---|---|---|
| Tax audit report (Form 3CA/3CB-3CD) — "specified date" under Section 44AB | 30 September 2026 | **21 October 2026** |
| ITR filing — audit cases (Section 44AB) | 31 October 2026 | **21 November 2026** |
| ITR filing — transfer pricing cases (Form 3CEB applicable) | 30 November 2026 | not extended by that circular — check |
| ITR filing — non-audit individuals (ITR-1/ITR-2) | 31 July 2026 | — |
| ITR filing — non-audit business/professional (ITR-3/ITR-4, no audit) | 31 August 2026 | — |
| Belated return | 31 December 2026 | — |
| Revised return | 31 March 2027 | — |

**If you are running this skill after 21 October 2026** for an AY 2026-27 audit, the report is late. Section 271B
penalty can apply (0.5% of turnover/gross receipts, capped at Rs. 1,50,000) unless there is reasonable cause under
section 273B. Flag this in the hand-off message; do not compute the penalty.

**Revised tax audit report:** Rule 6G(3) of the Income-tax Rules, 1962 allows a revised report where a payment
after the audit (e.g. a 43B item) or a change in law changes what was reported. Note this as an option in the
hand-off when a 43B item is paid after the report date.

**Using 43B dates in clause 26:** the "paid on or before the due date of return" test in clause 26 uses the return
due date under section 139(1) as extended — for audit cases this is now 21 November 2026, not 31 October.

## 16. UDIN (CA's own compliance step, not computed by this skill)

- UDIN must be generated on the ICAI UDIN portal within **60 days** of signing the audit report/certificate.
- This is the CA's own professional compliance obligation after the workpaper is finalized and signed — the skill doesn't need to compute anything here, just worth the CA keeping in mind as a downstream step.

this priority order — most likely to change first:
